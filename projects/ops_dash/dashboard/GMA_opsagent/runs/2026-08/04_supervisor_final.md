# Genesis Monthly Executive Insights

## Method and Limitations

- **Reporting month:** August 2026 (2026-08-01).
- **Competitive universe:** Genesis's represented competitive segments; SOI and SOM use the same aligned segment denominator. They are not shares of the entire luxury category.
- **Data coverage and material caveats:** Frozen national and seven-region inventory/retail metrics, national model incentives, segment peer benchmarks, price-band/trim inventory, and dealer inventory locations. The package is a one-month snapshot with supplied M/M and Y/Y deltas, so it supports diagnosis rather than causal proof. Incentives are national only; trim-level retail sales, transaction prices, dealer sales, leads, media exposure, inventory age, and program-effective dates are absent. No external research was used because no event-specific hypothesis met the defined research threshold.

## Executive Summary

1. **Genesis has the largest availability-to-retail imbalance among the named premium peers.**
   - **Observation:** Genesis held 7.6% SOI versus 4.6% SOM, a 1.66x ratio. The ratio exceeds BMW (1.38x), Mercedes-Benz (1.35x), Audi (1.19x), and Lexus (0.70x). In August, SOI rose 0.45pp while SOM rose 0.14pp, widening the ratio 0.05x even as Genesis retail sales increased 11.0% M/M.
   - **Interpretation:** **Fact** — Genesis has a higher share of represented-segment inventory than retail sales. **Hypothesis** — the immediate issue is converting or reallocating existing availability, not a broad national inventory shortage. August sales growth is encouraging, but it did not close the relative availability gap.
   - **Implication:** Use 1.66x as the brand diagnostic, but target the response by nameplate and region; do not treat aggregate inventory growth or a blanket incentive action as the default solution.
   - **Confidence:** High
   - **Evidence:** [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | Genesis | National | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: competitor_efficiency.csv | Genesis, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Audi, Lexus | National | 2026-08-01]

2. **The August incentive reset lowered spend, but Genesis remains above its inventory-weighted peer benchmark.**
   - **Observation:** Brand incentive PNV declined 25.7% M/M to $5,412, while the inventory-weighted incentive index declined 31.5 points to 115.3. An index above 100 means Genesis still exceeds its Cox segment benchmark. At the same time, the brand SOI/SOM ratio worsened 0.05x M/M.
   - **Interpretation:** **Fact** — Genesis is less incentive-intensive than in July but remains above peer on an inventory-weighted basis. **Hypothesis** — this rules out a simple explanation that August ratio deterioration was caused by more incentive spending; it does not prove the lower program is either helping or hurting conversion.
   - **Implication:** Evaluate incentive effectiveness at model and regional level, especially where excess availability is highest. Protect support only where it is associated with incremental conversion; do not restore broad spend based on a one-month outcome.
   - **Confidence:** High
   - **Evidence:** [Evidence: cox_incentives.csv | Genesis | National | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | Genesis | National | 2026-08-01]

3. **GV70 is the brand's largest-volume balanced platform, making it the right controlled test—not the primary outlet for excess supply.**
   - **Observation:** GV70 carried 8,465 average units and 2,858 retail sales, about 40% of Genesis inventory. Its 9.1% SOI and 9.2% SOM produce a near-balanced 0.98x ratio. Inventory was essentially flat (-0.2%) while retail sales rose 6.7% M/M; meanwhile its incentive index fell 46.0 points to 83.2, below the relevant peer benchmark.
   - **Interpretation:** **Fact** — GV70 is not currently driving the national availability imbalance. **Hypothesis** — the lower-support position has not yet impaired its August conversion, but a one-month result cannot demonstrate that the incentive change caused the sales result.
   - **Implication:** Preserve the current supply position and test the lower incentive stance by region with leading indicators. A small deterioration in GV70 conversion would materially affect the brand because of its scale.
   - **Confidence:** Medium
   - **Evidence:** [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | GV70 | National | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: cox_incentives.csv | GV70 | National | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: cox_segment_incentives.csv | Genesis GV70, BMW X3 | Near Luxury SUV | 2026-08-01]

4. **G80 is the clearest conventional nameplate with retail share ahead of availability, while G70 and G90 require a conversion diagnosis rather than more national support.**
   - **Observation:** G80 ran at 0.86x SOI/SOM: inventory increased 18.5% M/M, but sales increased 36.8% and retail share increased 1.45pp. Conversely, G70 ran at 1.27x with the highest Genesis incentive index (212.4), and G90 ran at 1.41x while August sales fell 5.2% M/M.
   - **Interpretation:** **Fact** — supply-sales balance differs sharply across conventional nameplates. **Hypothesis** — G80 may be constrained by availability in productive markets, whereas G70 and G90 need diagnosis of offer, mix, positioning, or local conversion before increased national support is justified.
   - **Implication:** Build a model-by-region allocation and conversion view before moving inventory. Prioritize an availability check for G80, and an incentive/retail-mix audit for G70 and G90.
   - **Confidence:** Medium
   - **Evidence:** [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | G80, G70, G90 | National | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: cox_incentives.csv | G80, G70, G90 | National | 2026-08-01]

5. **Southern and Western are different versions of the same regional overhang and should not receive the same response.**
   - **Observation:** Southern's SOI/SOM was 1.94x (11.7% SOI / 6.0% SOM); inventory share increased 1.13pp M/M while retail share declined 0.27pp. Western had the highest ratio at 1.97x (6.2% / 3.2%), but its retail share improved 0.48pp while inventory declined 1.9% M/M. Mountain States was relatively tighter at 1.27x.
   - **Interpretation:** **Fact** — both Southern and Western have excess availability relative to retail share, but their current-month trajectories differ. **Hypothesis** — Southern is the more immediate allocation/demand-capture concern; Western may be recovering from a pre-existing mix or dealer-concentration imbalance.
   - **Implication:** Start the dealer/DMA investigation in Southern and validate model mix in Western. Avoid a national action that could worsen conditions in tighter regions such as Mountain States.
   - **Confidence:** High
   - **Evidence:** [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | Genesis | Southern, Western, Mountain States | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: dealer_inventory.csv | Genesis dealers | United States | 2026-08-01]

## Model-Level Dynamics

### G70

- **Performance diagnosis:** G70 is over-available at 1.27x despite its 90.6 MSRP index and the highest Genesis incentive position ($7,817 PNV; 212.4 index); sales were down 24.6% Y/Y. [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | G70 | National | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: cox_incentives.csv | G70 | National | 2026-08-01]
- **Opportunity, risk, or targeted investigation:** Audit whether the high incentive is buying incremental retail conversion by market, or merely supporting aged inventory / unfavorable mix, before extending the program.

### G80

- **Performance diagnosis:** G80 is under-available at 0.86x, with sales (+36.8% M/M) growing faster than inventory (+18.5%); its incentive index remains high at 163.7 even after a 46.9-point decline. [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | G80 | National | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: cox_incentives.csv | G80 | National | 2026-08-01]
- **Opportunity, risk, or targeted investigation:** Verify whether this is sustained demand rather than delivery timing; if confirmed, protect allocation in its strongest regions before adding incremental national incentive support.

### G90

- **Performance diagnosis:** G90 ran at 1.41x, with August sales down 5.2% M/M and 17.9% Y/Y; inventory was down 1.4% M/M but up 43.9% Y/Y. Its incentive index is below peer at 90.0. [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | G90 | National | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: cox_incentives.csv | G90 | National | 2026-08-01]
- **Opportunity, risk, or targeted investigation:** Diagnose dealer, trim, and metro mix before interpreting below-peer incentive support as a reason to discount further; the low unit base makes percentage signals unstable.

### GV60

- **Performance diagnosis:** GV60's 0.44x ratio indicates retail share above inventory share, but it sits on only 162 average units and 100 retail sales; both inventory and sales are down roughly 41% Y/Y. [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | GV60 | National | 2026-08-01]
- **Opportunity, risk, or targeted investigation:** Confirm whether the ratio represents supply constraint or a contracting/volatile base before reallocating product or changing incentives; the 54.6 incentive index alone is not decisive. ### GV70

### GV70 EV

### GV80

- **Performance diagnosis:** GV80 is modestly over-available at 1.09x, with a 105.3 MSRP index; retail sales rose 14.1% M/M versus a 5.4% inventory increase, while its incentive index fell to 107.7. [Evidence: operational_metrics.csv | GV80 | National | 2026-08-01] [Evidence: cox_incentives.csv | GV80 | National | 2026-08-01]

## Investigate Next

1. **Question:** Which models, DMAs, and dealers produce Southern's 1.94x ratio, and is the driver arrivals, lead generation, sales conversion, or product mix?
   - **Why it could change a decision:** It determines whether to reallocate inventory, adjust retailer execution/marketing, or deploy targeted incentives rather than make a national intervention.
   - **Missing evidence:** Dealer/DMA sales, vehicle arrivals, days-to-turn, inventory age, incentive redemption, lead funnel, and local media exposure for August plus at least three prior months.

2. **Question:** Does GV70's below-peer incentive index preserve conversion in every region, or does national aggregation conceal markets where share is weakening?
   - **Why it could change a decision:** GV70's scale means even a modest regional deterioration can shift the brand result, while a sustained lower-support posture could improve commercial efficiency.
   - **Missing evidence:** Model-by-region incentive program timing, retail sales, retail share, lead-to-sale conversion, transaction price, and days-to-turn.

3. **Question:** Is G80 genuinely supply constrained, and where can incremental inventory generate incremental retail without more incentive?
   - **Why it could change a decision:** A targeted allocation move could be more efficient than adding national incentive spend if G80's 0.86x ratio reflects real unmet demand.
   - **Missing evidence:** Order/lead flow, wait time, days-to-turn, lost-sales reasons, dealer inventory age, and model-by-region sales across multiple months.

4. **Question:** Where do GV80's excess units sit by trim and advertised-price band, and does its 105.3 MSRP index inhibit conversion in those pockets?
   - **Why it could change a decision:** It separates an allocation/mix issue from a price or offer issue and determines whether a targeted incentive is warranted.
   - **Missing evidence:** Trim-level retail sales, transaction and advertised prices, offers redeemed, inventory age, and competitor trim mix by market.

5. **Question:** Are GV60 and GV70 EV ratios economically comparable to their true competitive sets?
   - **Why it could change a decision:** Resolving a potentially volatile denominator or timing issue prevents erroneous EV allocation or incentive decisions.
   - **Missing evidence:** Segment-membership audit, named inventory and sales denominator components, model-year mapping, dealer availability, and at least three months of model-level retail history.
